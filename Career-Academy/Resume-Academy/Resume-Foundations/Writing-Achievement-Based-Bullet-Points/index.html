Section 1 of 6

Duty vs. Achievement

A duty explains what the job required. An achievement explains how effectively the candidate performed it.

15%
Progress

Duty Statement

Answered customer calls and resolved issues.

It describes a normal responsibility.

Achievement Statement

Resolved 80+ daily customer inquiries while maintaining 96% quality and reducing repeat contacts 18%.

It shows volume, quality, and measurable impact.

Core principle: Duties describe the role. Achievements differentiate the candidate.

Section 2 of 6

The Achievement Bullet Formula

1. Action

What did you do?

2. Task

What did you handle?

3. Method

How did you do it?

4. Result

What changed?

Formula in action

Reduced repeat contacts by improving case documentation and follow-up workflows, cutting rework 18%.

Flexible framework: Not every bullet needs every element. Use the strongest truthful evidence available.

Section 3 of 6

Where Metrics Come From

Volume

Calls, claims, orders, customers, projects.

Quality

Accuracy, QA, CSAT, compliance.

Time

Turnaround, response time, time saved.

Money

Revenue, savings, collections, budget.

Scale

Team size, locations, systems, accounts.

Outcome

Retention, conversion, resolution, growth.

No percentage available? Use credible scope such as team size, account volume, project count, or transaction volume.
