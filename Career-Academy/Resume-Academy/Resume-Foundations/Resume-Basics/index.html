Section 2 of 7

What Is a Resume?

A resume is a targeted professional marketing document—not a complete autobiography.

Progress25%
ConceptPurpose

The definition

A resume is a concise document that presents the qualifications most relevant to a specific opportunity. It usually includes professional experience, skills, achievements, education, and credentials.

The resume is selective. It does not need to include every task, every job, or every detail from a person’s life.

The goal

The immediate goal is to help the employer answer one question:

“Does this candidate appear qualified enough to move forward?”

For most jobs, moving forward means an interview, recruiter screen, assessment, or hiring-manager review.

Where the resume fits

Job SearchFind a suitable opening
→
ResumeShow relevant value
→
InterviewEvaluate fit more deeply
Recruiter perspective: A recruiter may review many applicants for one role. The resume should make relevant qualifications easy to identify quickly.
🦋
Professor Butterfly

Think of the resume as a bridge. It connects your experience to the employer’s need. The stronger and clearer that connection is, the easier it is for the employer to justify moving you forward.

Section 3 of 7

Resume vs. Job Application

These documents work together, but they serve different purposes.

Progress40%
ConceptAccuracy

Resume

Purpose: Market relevant qualifications.
Content: Selected experience, achievements, skills, education, and credentials.
Strategy: Tailored to the target job.
Format: Designed for clarity, hierarchy, and impact.
Length: Usually concise and selective.

Job Application

Purpose: Create a standardized employer record.
Content: Complete dates, employers, duties, contact information, and required disclosures.
Strategy: Answer every required field accurately.
Format: Controlled by the employer’s application system.
Length: Determined by the questions asked.
Consistency matters: Employer names, job titles, dates, and education should not conflict across the resume and application. Differences can create credibility or verification concerns.
🦋
Professor Butterfly

Your resume can be selective, but it must still be truthful. The job application may request a fuller history. Tailoring means emphasizing relevance—not changing facts.

Section 4 of 7

The Three Main Resume Formats

The format determines how information is organized and what the recruiter notices first.

Progress55%
ConceptStructure

Reverse Chronological Resume

This format organizes professional experience from the most recent role backward. It is the most widely recognized and usually the safest choice when the candidate has a clear, relevant work history.

Jordan Ellis
Customer Success Specialist
Professional Experience

2024–Present · Current Role

2021–2024 · Previous Role

2019–2021 · Earlier Role

Best for
Steady employment, similar target roles, visible progression, and recent relevant experience.
Watch for
Large gaps or frequent short roles may be more noticeable because dates are prominent.

Combination Resume

This format combines a strong skills or qualifications section with a clear reverse-chronological work history. It helps a candidate emphasize transferable strengths without hiding employment details.

Taylor Morgan
Corporate Training Specialist
Relevant Qualifications

Facilitation · Curriculum Development · Coaching

Professional Experience

2022–Present · Educator

2019–2022 · Training Assistant

Best for
Career changes, transferable skills, diverse experience, and candidates who need both skills emphasis and transparent chronology.
Watch for
The page can become crowded if the skills section repeats the same information found in experience.

Functional Resume

This format emphasizes skill categories more than employers and dates. It may help in limited situations, but many recruiters prefer visible chronology and may question what the format is trying to hide.

Morgan Reed
Operations Support Professional
Customer Service Skills
Administrative Skills
May help with
Severe gaps, limited traditional experience, project-based work, or highly unusual career circumstances.
Watch for
Recruiters and ATS workflows may still expect employer names, titles, and dates. Use cautiously.
Practical recommendation: Reverse chronological is appropriate for most candidates. Combination is often the best alternative for career changers. Functional should be used only when there is a clear strategic reason.

Section 5 of 7

What to Gather Before Writing

Strong resume writing depends on evidence. Gather the facts before trying to write polished language.

Progress70%
ConceptEvidence

Employment facts

Employer names, job titles, locations, start dates, end dates, promotions, and major responsibilities.

Achievements and metrics

Volume handled, quality scores, revenue, savings, time reductions, customer satisfaction, accuracy, deadlines, team size, or project scope.

Tools and systems

CRM platforms, office software, ticketing systems, healthcare systems, machinery, technical tools, or industry platforms.

Education and credentials

Degrees, diplomas, certifications, licenses, relevant training, continuing education, and expiration dates when applicable.

🦋
Professor Butterfly

Do not wait until you are writing the bullet point to search for proof. Build an evidence bank first. The more specific your evidence is, the stronger your resume can become.

Preparation checklist

1
List every employer, title, and date range.
2
Write down major duties in plain language.
3
Identify achievements, improvements, recognition, and difficult problems solved.
4
Estimate measurable scope: customers, calls, claims, orders, projects, dollars, percentages, or time.
5
Collect tools, systems, education, licenses, and certifications.
6
Review the target job description for required skills and keywords.
