Lesson 1: What Customer Success Actually Does

Customer Success is a proactive business function focused on helping customers achieve value over time.

Learn + Practice
🛎️

Customer Service

Responds when a customer has a question, request, or problem.

Primary outcome: resolution and satisfaction
🛠️

Customer Support

Provides product or technical assistance when something is not working.

Primary outcome: product functionality
🌱

Customer Success

Guides customers toward adoption, value, retention, renewal, and growth.

Primary outcome: long-term customer outcomes
Professor Reeva's Note: Customer Success is not “customer service with a nicer title.” The work shifts from reacting to isolated issues toward managing the health and value of the full customer relationship.

Practice: Identify the Function

A customer has purchased project-management software. You schedule onboarding, review usage after 30 days, identify underused features, and recommend training before renewal.

Lesson 7: Explain the Transition in Your Cover Letter

The cover letter should connect your existing customer work to the proactive, outcome-focused nature of Customer Success.

Complete Sample

Dear Hiring Manager,

I am applying for the Customer Success Associate position with a background in high-volume customer service, account support, product education, and escalation resolution. Throughout my experience, I have helped customers understand products, navigate account concerns, and regain confidence after difficult interactions—responsibilities that directly support the relationship-building and customer-value focus of Customer Success.

In my current role, I resolve more than 70 customer inquiries each day while maintaining 95% quality scores. I document customer needs in Salesforce, coordinate solutions with billing and technical teams, and follow up after complex cases to confirm resolution. These experiences have strengthened my ability to identify customer goals, communicate clearly, and protect long-term relationships.

I am particularly interested in moving into Customer Success because I want to contribute beyond individual issue resolution. I am prepared to help customers onboard effectively, adopt the right features, remain engaged, and recognize continued value from the product. I would welcome the opportunity to bring my customer advocacy, CRM documentation, and cross-functional problem-solving skills to your team.

Sincerely,
Jordan Taylor

Lesson 11: Think Like the Recruiter

Review three career changers. Choose the candidate with the strongest credible evidence—not the most aggressive title claims.

Recruiter Mode

Candidate A

Lists five years of customer service duties but no metrics, CRM tools, follow-up, or examples of customer education.

Candidate B

Shows CRM documentation, customer education, escalations, cross-functional resolution, 95% quality, and proactive follow-up.

Candidate C

Changed every job title to Customer Success Manager but provides no evidence of onboarding, adoption, retention, or renewals.
